## Scope

This policy covers business travel and work expenses for all employees. Spend company money as carefully as you would your own. When something is not covered here, ask Finance before you spend, not after.

## Booking travel

Book flights, hotels and rental cars through the TripDesk travel portal, so that we can reach you in an emergency and track spending. Trips need your manager's approval in TripDesk before you book. Book flights at least 14 days before departure when you can.

## Flights

Fly economy on flights under 6 hours. Premium economy is allowed on flights of 6 hours or more. Business class needs approval from the Chief Financial Officer. Paid seat upgrades and airport lounge passes are not reimbursed, but one checked bag per trip is.

## Hotels

The nightly limit, before taxes, is $210 in most cities and $290 in high-cost cities: New York, San Francisco, Boston, Seattle and Washington, DC. When you attend a conference, stay in the conference hotel even if it costs more than the limit.

## Meals

The daily meal allowance for domestic travel is $65: up to $15 for breakfast, $20 for lunch and $30 for dinner. On the first and last day of a trip, you can claim 75% of the daily allowance. Meals provided by a conference or included in a hotel rate cannot also be claimed. Alcohol is not reimbursed, except at client dinners that a vice president approved in advance.

## International travel

Trips outside the United States and Canada need approval from your vice president as well as your manager, at least 30 days before departure. The daily meal allowance abroad is $85. Passport and visa fees for business trips are reimbursed. The company's travel insurance covers every trip booked through TripDesk, including medical emergencies and evacuation abroad, so you do not need to buy your own. Before you go, check with IT which devices you may take; some countries restrict encrypted laptops, and IT can lend you a travel laptop with no customer data on it.

## Travel safety

If something goes wrong on a trip, such as a cancelled flight, a natural disaster or a medical emergency, call the 24-hour travel assistance line printed on the back of your TripDesk itinerary. It can rebook travel, find a doctor and contact your manager for you. Tell your manager about any change that keeps you away longer than planned.

## Ground transportation

Use public transit, taxis or ride-hailing between airports, hotels and meetings. Rental cars should be compact or midsize. When you drive your own car for business, claim mileage at $0.67 per mile instead of fuel costs. The commute between your home and your usual workplace cannot be claimed.

## Receipts

Keep receipts for every expense of $25 or more, and for every hotel stay whatever it costs. Hotel receipts must be itemized. Photograph receipts with the expense app; you do not need to keep the paper copies.

## Submitting expenses

Submit your expense report within 30 days of the end of a trip, or of the purchase for other expenses. Approved expenses are paid with the next regular payroll. Expenses submitted more than 90 days after they were incurred are not reimbursed.

## Company cards

Employees who travel 4 or more times a year can get a company credit card. The card is for business expenses only, and any personal charge must be repaid within 30 days.

## Not reimbursable

- Traffic and parking fines
- Personal entertainment, such as movies or minibar items
- Pet care, child care and house sitting
- Lost or stolen personal belongings
- Upgrades of any kind without approval
