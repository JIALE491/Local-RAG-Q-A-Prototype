## Purpose

We want new parents to have time with their child without worrying about their job or their pay. This policy covers birth, adoption, foster placement and surrogacy, and applies equally to parents of every gender.

## Eligibility

Full-time employees, and part-time employees scheduled for 20 or more hours a week, are eligible after 6 months of continuous employment. Employees who are not yet eligible when their child arrives become eligible when they reach 6 months, and can take their leave then, within the time limit below.

## How much leave

- Primary caregivers: 16 weeks of fully paid leave
- Secondary caregivers: 6 weeks of fully paid leave

Each parent decides whether they are the primary or the secondary caregiver. If both parents work here, both can be primary caregivers.

## When to take leave

Leave must be taken within 12 months of the birth, adoption or placement. It can be taken in one block or split into two blocks, each at least 2 weeks long.

## Giving notice

Tell your manager and the People Team at least 30 days before you expect your leave to start, or as soon as you can if the timing is unexpected. Submit the parental leave form in the Waypoint HR portal so that payroll and benefits are updated.

## Pay and benefits during leave

Your full base pay continues through payroll during paid leave. Health, dental and vision coverage continue on the same terms, and you keep earning vacation and sick leave while you are away.

## Before the birth

Time off for medical reasons before a birth, such as doctor-ordered bed rest, is covered by short-term disability rather than by this policy, and it does not reduce your parental leave. The Benefits Overview explains short-term disability.

## Phased return to work

After primary caregiver leave, you can return on a reduced schedule of 60% of your usual hours for up to 4 weeks, at full pay. Agree on the schedule with your manager before your leave ends.

## Returning to your job

You return to the same job, or to an equivalent job with the same pay, level and location.

## Support at work

Every site has a private lactation room with a lock, a chair, a refrigerator and a power outlet. Lactation breaks are paid and do not need to be scheduled in advance.

## Unpaid extension

After paid leave ends, you can ask for up to 8 more weeks of unpaid leave. Your manager and the People Team decide based on the needs of the team, and your benefits continue during the unpaid extension.
